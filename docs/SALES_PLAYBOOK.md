# TradeFlow Sales Playbook

**Purpose:** the call-time layer. `knowledge/TradeFlowContext.md` says what is true about the
product; `knowledge/SaadKhaja.md` says how to sell; this file decides what the founder actually
does on a dial. Built for the coach's system prompt.

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Status:** v0, needs field validation

---

## 1. The strategic problem nobody has said out loud yet

The founder is selling a **missed-call recovery tool for people who are impossible to reach by
phone.**

That is not a coincidence — it is the definition of the ICP. A solo tradesperson misses calls
*because* they are under a sink, on a ladder, driving between jobs, or wrist-deep in a panel.
The product exists precisely because this person cannot answer the phone.

**So cold calling this ICP is fighting the exact problem the product solves.** Connect rates
will be poor for a reason that has nothing to do with caller ID, script quality, or the offer.
The largest cause is that the target is, by definition, holding a wrench.

This has three consequences that change the whole go-to-market:

1. **A low connect rate is not evidence the offer is wrong.** Do not conclude from 100 dials
   with 12 connects that TradeFlow doesn't sell. The correct read is: the channel needs the
   right *shape* for this ICP.
2. **Every missed call is data, not a failure.** It is live proof the prospect has the problem,
   and it is the natural permission to follow up (SaadKhaja.md §3.3).
3. **The best outreach motion is the product's own mechanism.** See §2.

## 2. The self-demonstrating outreach motion (the strongest idea in this file)

**Call. Let it miss. Then do exactly what TradeFlow does.**

Deliberately call during working hours, expecting no answer. When it rings out, that is the
setup, not the failure. You now have:

- **proof** the prospect misses calls while working — witnessed, not hypothesised;
- **a reason to follow up** that is honest, specific, and welcome (SaadKhaja.md §3.3: they
  remain your ICP regardless of timing);
- **the demo itself** — because what you send next is the product.

The follow-up text mirrors TradeFlow's own first message:

> Hi Mike — I called a few minutes ago and you were probably on a job. That's sort of the point:
> I couldn't reach you, exactly like your customers can't. I built an app that texts them back
> from your own number when you can't pick up. What I just sent you is what it would send them.
> Worth two minutes this evening? Reply STOP to opt out.

**Why this works:** it converts the ICP's single hardest trait — unavailability — into the
demonstration. It is proof with no testimonial required, which matters enormously because
TradeFlow has **no proof assets at all** (`TradeFlowContext.md` §9), and SaadKhaja.md §9.5 says
a belief-break without proof behind it has nothing to stand on.

**It also inverts the 5-minute rule in the founder's favour.** SaadKhaja.md §8.2: the moment of
inquiry is the moment of highest probability of sale, and speed is the whole point. When a
tradesperson *calls back* the founder's missed number, that callback is a pre-qualified,
self-selected lead inside its own 5-minute window. Answer it, always.

### ⚠️ Compliance before scaling this
- Cold **texting** a business is not the same as cold calling it. TCPA exposure on SMS is real
  and the penalties are per-message. B2B has narrower carve-outs than many assume.
- **Get the opt-out right from message one** — an explicit STOP line, honoured permanently.
- Automated SMS from a personal number at volume can attract carrier A2P filtering (§6 below).
- **This motion is not cleared for volume.** Run it manually, at small numbers, on the founder's
  own number, and get a compliance opinion before it becomes a campaign.

## 3. Call architecture for this product

Adapted from SaadKhaja.md §9, re-sequenced for a $599 one-time sale to an owner-operator.
Target length: **10–15 minutes.** This is a short sale; do not run a 45-minute enterprise
discovery.

| # | Stage | Time | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Open** | 30s | Honesty-first hard cold open. **Not** the old-friend pattern (§11.3). |
| 2 | **Permission + context** | 30s | §9.2 verbatim structure. Ask permission to ask questions. |
| 3 | **NEED discovery** | 3–5 min | **The whole call lives here.** See §3.1. |
| 4 | **The delta — live** | 2 min | ROI calculator *with* them, not at them. |
| 5 | **Objections** | 2–5 min | §4 map. Agree → why → core problem. |
| 6 | **Belief-break → proof** | 2 min | Live demo. §3.2. |
| 7 | **Method** | 1–2 min | The six-step flow, plainly. |
| 8 | **Price** | 20s | Confident and assertive tonality. Then stop talking. |
| 9 | **Close or book** | 1 min | Either close, or a specific booked callback. Never "I'll send some info." |

### 3.1 Stage 3 — the discovery drill that actually works

**The trap:** asking *"do you miss calls?"* is useless. Every owner-operator will say no,
because they genuinely believe they call everyone back. And they're nearly right — they do call
back. **They just call back late, and late is the whole problem.**

So do not ask whether they miss calls. Ask about the *sequence*, and let them compute the loss.

1. **Situation.** *"When your phone rings and you're under a sink or on a roof — what actually
   happens?"* They will describe letting it ring and calling back later. **That is the entire
   problem, stated by them, unprompted.**
2. **Timing.** *"How long usually before you get back to them?"* Answers will be hours. Some will
   say tomorrow.
3. **The bridge** (SaadKhaja.md §8.2, usable as general principle, not as a TradeFlow claim):
   *"Here's the thing I've learned talking to trades — the moment somebody calls, that's the
   moment they're most likely to buy. Call them four hours later and they've already got
   someone else on the way, or the emergency's passed, or they just don't care any more. Does
   that sound familiar?"*
4. **The compounding question.** *"So how many rings out in a week, do you reckon? Say it out
   loud — even a rough number."* Then: *"And of those, how many do you think you actually get
   back to?"*
5. **The three-layers-of-why drill** (§4) — *"why does that bother you?"* → *"why does that
   matter?"* → until you reach money, time, or stress. **That is where the real motivation is.**
6. **Then, and only then, the delta** (§3.3).

**Do not pitch inside stage 3.** §3.1's rule holds: the moment the prospect hears a pitch, the
honest answers stop.

### 3.2 Stage 6 — belief-break and proof, for a product with no testimonials

SaadKhaja.md §9.5 requires **belief-break → proof → method.** TradeFlow has no testimonials,
no case studies, no before/after numbers. **Do not fake any.** Instead:

- **Belief-break question:** *"How long do you reckon you've got before a caller tries the next
  plumber on the list?"* They will guess generously. The honest answer, stated as general
  observation rather than as a TradeFlow statistic: *"Most people are already dialling someone
  else before you've put your tools down."*
- **Proof = the live demo.** Show the real app on the real phone. Send a real text. Let the text
  arrive in the room. Watch their face — that is the proof, and it is *stronger* than a
  testimonial because they can verify it themselves.
- **If the demo isn't possible on the call**, run the ROI calculator with their numbers instead.
  Never substitute a claim for a demonstration.

### 3.3 Stage 4 — the delta must be computed by *them*

SaadKhaja.md §11.1: a large value delta makes selling easy, **but asserting the delta creates
resistance whereas helping them calculate it creates the sale.**

Practical instruction: open the website ROI calculator, hand them the inputs verbally, and let
**them** supply average job value, missed calls per week, and conversion rate. Then read the
outputs back. Their arithmetic in their own head is worth more than any figure the founder states.

**Mandatory framing [NEVER]:** it estimates *potential gross job revenue*. It is not guaranteed
income, not profit, and it ignores costs, margins, cancellations, and seasonality. Say so out
loud — every time. A number sold as a promise becomes a refund demand.

### 3.4 Stage 8 — price delivery

$599 one-time is **good news** in this conversation and should be delivered as such, not
apologised for. Use **confident and assertive** tonality (SaadKhaja.md §6.2): price is delivered
flatly, without hedging, without upward inflection. Then **stop talking.** §9.5: shut up and let
them ask the buying question.

Frame against the alternative they already understand — not against competitor pricing
(illustrative only, §3.2 of the context file), but against **one job**: *"It's $599, once. What's
your average job worth?"* Let them do the division.

## 4. Objection map

Every entry: what they say → what it usually means → the move. Framework references are to
`SaadKhaja.md`.

### 4.1 "I don't miss calls. I call everyone back."
**Means:** they're not lying — they're measuring the wrong thing.
**Move:** don't argue. §5.1 — never reject the premise. *"I'm sure you do. Most of the guys I
talk to do call back — they just do it three hours later. How long is it usually for you?"*
Then run §3.1. **This is a need-gate failure, not an objection. Do not pitch past it.**

### 4.2 "How did you get my number?" / hostility
**Means:** alarm, not necessarily rejection. §3.1 notes the response is a reflex.
**Move:** §3.2's recovery. Answer immediately and honestly — *"Fair question. You're on
[Google Business / a trade directory] — it's public. If you want, I'll make sure nobody from my
side contacts you again."* Offer the exit; it usually converts resistance into permission. Then
re-open honestly.

### 4.3 "Not a good time — I'm on a job."
**Means:** often literally true. This ICP is physically busy.
**Move:** §3.1's pre-objection handle exists for this, but if it lands anyway: agree, do not
push, and **book a specific time.** §3.4: never "when suits you?" — give two slots. *"Totally
get it. I'll call you at 6, or does 7 tomorrow work better?"* Do **not** launch into a pitch to
someone holding a torch.

### 4.4 "$599 is a lot of money."
**Means:** either access or allocation. §5.3 says these are different and must be split.
**Move:** first, the isolate (§5.3): *"Fair enough. If price weren't a factor, is this something
you'd want running?"* → then the split: *"Is it that the money isn't there right now, or that
you're not sure it's worth it?"*
- **Allocation** → educate and show the delta. The ROI calculator exists for exactly this.
- **Access** → §5.3 says **disqualify.** Do not pressure. Offer to stay in touch (§3.3).
**Watch for:** this is frequently a **buying objection** (§5.2) — if they've already asked
"how do I get it", they're haggling, not declining. Stay warm and don't concede price.

### 4.5 "It's not on the Play Store? I have to install an APK?" ⚠️ **the real #1 blocker**
**Means:** a lifetime of being told not to do exactly this. Android has trained them well.
**Move:** never dismiss it, never say "just ignore the warning" — the context file forbids that.
Answer the *why* first (§9.2 context-before-questions applies to objections too): *"Right — and
the reason is that Google won't let any app read your call log and send texts from your SIM,
which is exactly what this needs to do. So it's a direct install instead."*
Then reduce the effort (§7 component 5): *"I'll walk you through it line by line while we're
on the call. Takes about two minutes and you don't have to work anything out."*
**Also disclose honestly:** it will say the file is from outside Google Play; Android 14+ may ask
you to allow restricted settings; some phones restrict background apps. If they're not willing to
do that, this genuinely isn't the right product for them — say so.

### 4.6 "Will this get my number flagged as spam?"
**Means:** an intelligent, legitimate fear. Their business number is their livelihood.
**Move:** **do not bluff this.** The honest position: texts go from *their own number* to *someone
who called them first*, which is a reply to an existing enquiry rather than bulk marketing — a
materially different thing from a blast. **But the carrier-filtering behaviour is not
established** (`TradeFlowContext.md` §10.11). So say exactly that: *"Here's what I know and
here's what I don't. It replies to people who called you — that's not the same as a marketing
blast. But I can't quote you carrier policy, and I'd rather test it on your number with you than
promise you something I haven't verified."*
**Then put it in the product/legal backlog.** This one can end the company if it goes wrong at
volume, and it is currently uninvestigated.

### 4.7 "Does it work with Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan?"
**Answer: no. There are no integrations.** The product is local-first with no internet permission.
**Move:** never fudge this. **But the honest answer is also a reframe:** *"No — and I'd rather
tell you that straight. It's not built to plug into your CRM, it's built to do the one thing
those don't: get in front of the customer in the first sixty seconds. You approve every job, and
it stays on your phone. If you need it to sync with your dispatch system, this isn't it."*
**Note:** someone running Jobber with a dispatch workflow is probably **not the ICP anyway** —
they're bigger than a solo operator. See §6.

### 4.8 "I already have an answering service / my wife answers the phones."
**Means:** they've already paid to solve this. §5.5 is written for exactly this.
**Move:** the two questions, in order — *"What are you getting for that?"* → *"Why did you go
with them, and what's not quite right about it?"* The second question is where the real answer
lives. Common findings: expensive, generic, doesn't know their trade, doesn't book, takes
messages they then have to chase.
**Do not attack the spouse or the service.** §5.1: agree first. *"Honestly, that's better than
most. So what does she do when she's out too?"*

### 4.9 "Is it AI?"
**Answer: no, and don't pretend otherwise.** The context file hard-blocks "AI-powered".
**Move:** this is a **selling point** for this ICP, not a concession. *"No. No AI, no cloud, no
monthly fee. It's rules on your phone, sending from your number, and you approve every booking
before it's real."* Solo tradespeople are frequently AI-sceptical and privacy-conscious; honesty
here builds credibility rather than losing it.
**⚠️ Coach rule:** never let the reasoning model behind the coach leak into the pitch. The
founder's internal tool is AI; the product is not. Never blur them.

### 4.10 "What if it texts something stupid to my customer?"
**Means:** fear of looking unprofessional — the real stakes for someone whose reputation is local.
**Move:** §7 component 6, reduce risk. Point at the design: it asks, it doesn't assert; the owner
approves every booking; they can see every message and take over at any point (human handoff is
built in). Offer the low-risk version: *"Run it for a week yourself. Watch every text it sends.
You'll see within about two calls whether it sounds like you."*

### 4.11 "I'm not very techy."
**Means:** genuine, and probably true.
**Move:** §7 component 5 — *reduce time and effort to zero.* *"You don't have to figure anything
out. I'll set it up with you on a call, and if anything goes wrong you ring me."* Make an explicit
commitment about **who they call when it breaks** — right now that is the founder personally, and
that is a real cost to promise (§7).

### 4.12 "Let me think about it / I'll talk to my wife."
**Means:** either a genuine nurture lead, or unspoken doubt. §9.4 says the wrong move here is
expensive in both directions.
**Move:** §9.7 — **do not let them leave without a specific booked time.** *"Of course. When's
good — tomorrow evening or Thursday morning?"* If they want to talk to a partner, that's a
justified timeline: treat it as a **nurture lead** (§9.4) and *do not press.* Come back with
patience, not pressure. Over-pressuring a nurture lead loses them permanently.
**Also flag:** a $599 decision genuinely does involve a spouse in a solo-trade household. This is
a legitimate gate, not a brush-off.

### 4.13 "What if I change phones / it stops working?"
**Move:** honest, per the context file: backup and restore exists, **and it exports a plain-text
file that isn't encrypted.** *"There's backup and restore, so a new phone is fine. One thing I'll
tell you straight: the backup file is plain text, so keep it somewhere you'd keep customer
records."*
**Do not call the backup encrypted.** It isn't, and the website's wording on this needs fixing
(`TradeFlowContext.md` §11.6).

### 4.14 "Can you do it cheaper?"
**Means:** §5.2 — usually a **buying objection**. Intent is already there; they want support.
**Move:** stay warm, don't concede. *"The price is the price — it's one payment, not a
subscription, so there's nothing to discount without me lying to you about the value."* Then
return to the delta and the close.
**Note:** the founder must decide **in advance** whether any discount exists and who authorises
it. It is currently `[UNKNOWN]` in the context file, and improvising one on a call devalues every
subsequent sale.

## 5. Tonality cheat-sheet for this call

From SaadKhaja.md §6.2, mapped to this product's call stages:

| Stage | Tonality |
| --- | --- |
| Open, 30s | Friendly and inviting — but **honest**, not faux-familiar |
| "What happens when the phone rings?" | **Calm and reassuring** — therapist mode. This is the pain-extraction voice |
| Hearing them describe frustration | **Empathetic** — "I get it, that's brutal" |
| "Here's the thing I've learned…" | Calm, low, slow. Let the pause do the work |
| Delivering $599 | **Confident and assertive.** Flat, no upward inflection, no hedge. Then silence |
| Objection handling | **Persuasive and compelling** — start low, rise slowly, **pause after "I understand…"** |
| Live demo | **Enthusiastic** — genuinely, because the demo is good |

**The authenticity constraint (§6.2) is a product requirement, not a nicety.** The coach must
suggest the tone that fits the moment — not a tone that isn't the founder's. A model-dictated
tone the founder can't carry will sound exactly like the telemarketer §3.1 warns about, and it
will do more damage than saying nothing.

## 6. Where this product genuinely struggles — say it internally, never at the prospect

Be clear-eyed about these. They shape targeting, expectations and product roadmap.

1. **No iPhone.** A meaningful share of the ICP carries an iPhone. There is no answer for them
   today. Do not attempt one.
2. **No integrations.** Bigger operators on Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan will reject it, and
   correctly — it doesn't fit their workflow. **This is a targeting constraint, not a lost sale.**
   Pre-qualify it in discovery: *"Do you use any dispatch software?"* Yes → probably not the ICP.
3. **APK sideloading is a genuine adoption barrier**, not a marketing inconvenience. Expect setup
   abandonment and provide real hand-holding. Budget founder time for it.
4. **No proof assets.** Zero testimonials, zero case studies. Every sale is currently a
   "be the first" sale, which is harder and slower than it will be in three months. The first 10
   customers should be asked for a testimonial *and* numbers, explicitly, in exchange for
   hands-on onboarding.
5. **No update path.** Direct APK distribution with no store listing means no obvious way to push
   fixes. Support burden accrues indefinitely. **This is a product problem worth solving early.**
6. **No recurring revenue.** $599 once, then the founder costs money every time a customer has a
   question. With §7 component 5's promise ("I'll set it up with you"), support becomes the
   largest hidden cost. Bound it deliberately.
7. **"Lifetime access" is undefined.** What it covers — devices, updates, support, major versions
   — is unresolved. It will be asked. Decide before it becomes a dispute.
8. **Refund policy is undefined.** High installation friction plus a $599 one-time payment is
   textbook chargeback territory. **Decide this before scaling.** Note SaadKhaja.md §7.1's
   anti-guarantee argument does *not* transfer from his recurring-delivery context — see §11.4
   of the knowledge file.
9. **Carrier filtering risk is uninvestigated** (§4.6). Potentially the most serious item here.

## 7. What to fix before volume

In rough priority order:

1. **Investigate carrier A2P filtering** for automated SMS from a personal US number (§4.6).
   This is the only item that could cause customer harm at scale.
2. **Decide the refund policy and the meaning of "lifetime access."** Both get asked on calls.
3. **Fix the website privacy wording** on backup encryption (`TradeFlowContext.md` §11.6).
4. **Produce an honest installation guide** with a version number, with screenshots for the
   Android versions actually tested. This directly addresses §4.5, the biggest blocker.
5. **Build a tested-device list.** "Which phones does it work on?" currently has no answer.
6. **Get the first 5–10 customers to record a testimonial and share their real numbers.**
   Everything in §4 gets easier with proof.
7. **Re-verify the ROI calculator** and label every figure as an estimate.
8. **Decide the discount policy** (§4.14) before it's improvised on a call.

## 8. What the coach should surface, in order, on a live call

The M1 suggestion contract, specialised for this product:

1. **Prioritise discovery over pitching, always.** If the call is before stage 4, the suggestion
   should almost always be a question (§3.5: never end on a statement).
2. **Never suggest a blocked claim** (§8.2 of the context file) — no AI, no integrations, no
   encrypted backup, no guaranteed numbers.
3. **Detect the objection type** before suggesting a response: need-gate failure, buying
   objection, allocation problem, access problem, trust problem (sideload), or nurture-lead
   mis-press. **These need different moves and the wrong one is expensive.**
4. **Flag when a prospect is out of ICP** — iPhone, dispatch software, no personal answering —
   and suggest an honest disqualification rather than a doomed pitch (§9.3). Disqualifying fast
   is a *feature*; it protects the founder's calendar and his delivery load (§7).
5. **Watch for the "asked but unanswered" question.** If the prospect asked something the
   knowledge base cannot answer — a tested-device list, carrier policy, an integration — the
   coach's job is to prompt an honest *"I don't know, let me find out"*, never an invention.
